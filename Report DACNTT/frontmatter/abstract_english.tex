\begin{center}
{\fontsize{15pt}{18pt}\selectfont \textbf{BUILDING A HYBRID RECOMMENDATION MODEL COMBINING MATRIX FACTORIZATION AND DEEP NEURAL NETWORKS}\par}
\vspace{0.5cm}
{\fontsize{16pt}{19.2pt}\selectfont \textbf{ABSTRACT}\par}
\end{center}
\vspace{0.2cm}

Matrix Factorization (MF) models customer--item interactions with the inner product of two latent vectors \parencite{koren2009matrix}: a linear combination, learned from customer and item IDs only. On fashion data -- extremely sparse, heavily skewed towards popular items and seasonal -- both are limitations. This project builds \textbf{NeuMF-F}, a hybrid model that keeps the two-branch structure of Neural Matrix Factorization \parencite{he2017ncf} -- a linear Generalized Matrix Factorization (GMF) branch and a non-linear Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) branch fused at the prediction layer -- but represents every customer and item by an ID embedding plus a projection of features: item attributes and text description, customer attributes, sales and time.

Experiments use the H\&M Personalized Fashion Recommendations dataset \parencite{hm2022kaggle} (31.8 million transactions). Two disjoint, reproducible customer samples are drawn by hashing: the development sample (21,599 customers) is used only for tuning on its validation set, and the holdout sample (21,351 customers) is used once, for the final evaluation. Each item is a product design (all colours merged); data are split by time; the $k$-core filter uses only data before the test cut-off; the candidate set contains the items with training data before the cut-off; all features are computed point-in-time, without future information. Fourteen models -- NeuMF-F, its GMF-F and MLP-F branches, the late-fusion LateFusion-F, ID-only NeuMF with its two branches, and seven baselines (Random, Most Popular, recent popularity, content-based, ItemKNN, UserKNN, BPR-MF) -- are tuned with the same budget. The final evaluation follows a pre-registered plan with a locked test set: full ranking, five seeds, per-user Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with bootstrap confidence intervals and Holm correction over a family of ten comparisons.

On the test set of the holdout sample (\VData{Btest}{users} customers), the proposed NeuMF-F reaches NDCG@10 = \VRes{NeuMFF}{NDCG10}, second of 14 models after LateFusion-F (\VRes{LateF}{NDCG10}), compared with \VRes{NeuMF}{NDCG10} for ID-only NeuMF, \VRes{BPR}{NDCG10} for BPR-MF, \VRes{UKNN}{NDCG10} for UserKNN and \VRes{RecPop}{NDCG10} for recent popularity. In the pre-registered family of ten comparisons, NeuMF-F significantly outperforms ID-only NeuMF (\VSig{1}{rel}), its MLP-F branch (\VSig{3}{rel}) and all five baselines of the family (by \VSig{7}{rel} to \VSig{9}{rel}); NeuMF-F is not significantly different from its GMF-F branch alone (\VSig{2}{rel}) or from LateFusion-F (\VSig{4}{rel}): the gain comes from the features -- time features matter most in the ablation -- rather than from how the two branches are fused. The four feature-based models keep their order and lead at $K = 5, 10, 15, 20$. A validation run on the Amazon Beauty dataset with the same code and configurations confirms the feature effect (NeuMF-F outperforms ID-only NeuMF by \ASig{1}{rel}), but there late fusion significantly outperforms NeuMF-F. Following the supervisor's advice, the baselines are grouped into single models, traditional machine learning and recommendation-specific models; in a supplementary analysis against a traditional hybrid, Hybrid-CBCF (content-based combined with user- and item-based collaborative filtering), NeuMF-F differs by \VSig{B1}{rel} on H\&M (\VSig{B1}{phraseEN}). The earlier development phase (ID-only NeuMF, evaluated on the development sample itself) is reported as development history: NeuMF did not outperform a tuned MF baseline, which is why the project turned to adding information rather than only model complexity. An inference-only demo (FastAPI) compares the models for individual customers and recommends popular items to new customers without purchase history.
